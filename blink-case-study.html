OVERVIEW

BLink is a campus events app for UCLA students to discover and post pop-ups, giveaways, and other events. I joined the BLink team as a designer through DevX, a product development club at UCLA, to design and market the app.

THE PROBLEM

The original prototypes for BLink had a blocky, simple design with inconsistent button designs and corner radii, which made the app look rudimentary and unfinished. Our product manager wanted the design to be more modern and wanted to revamp the entire visual design of the project.

RESEARCH

After identifying what needed to be changed about the current BLink design, our next step was establishing a new visual direction for the marketing and UX of the app.

INITIAL DESIGNS

We based much of our low-fidelity wireframing off of the original prototypes but added some pages that were missing initially, like the account dashboard, loading screen, and form pages.

HI-FI ITERATIONS

To finalize the theme of the app’s UX, we created several iterations of the core pages and presented them to our product manager and our peers in DevX for feedback.

FINAL DESIGN

TAKEAWAYS

Working cross-functionally: A constraint that limited our design work was understanding how the developers on the team would implement them. Some aspects that we included in our designs, like the glassy effect on the bottom nav bar, didn’t translate well when actually implemented. Once we gained a better understanding of the tech stack and process that the dev team used, we were able to tailor our designs to be achievable for the app’s initial release within our deadline.

Understanding the user: We initially included placeholders for photos for each pop-up or event, but as we experimented with using the app ourselves, we realized that the average user posting about a current pop-up likely wouldn’t want to take the time to take and upload a photo. Additionally, having photos could take away from the visual appeal of the UX and also left open the possibility for users to add inappropriate or irrelevant photos to the home page of the app. We ultimately removed the photo feature, which made our pages look much more streamlined and clean.

BONUS: PREVIEW PAGES & SOCIAL DESIGN

As designers that worked closely with the marketing team, we created much of the marketing and social materials for BLink in accordance with our app redesign to translate the new modern feel of the UX to our socials.

Tools used: Adobe Photoshop, Canva, Figma
