Culikid

Website & Information Architecture Redesign

Role

Product Designer

Timeline

May - Aug. 2026

Team

1 Product Manager
4 Developers
3 Designers

OVERVIEW

Culikid is a NYC nonprofit offering cooking classes for neurodivergent individuals to learn independence, develop motor skills, and build community. I worked as a volunteer through Develop For Good to redesign and restructure the Culikid site to reflect the organization’s mission.

THE PROBLEM

The existing Culikid site lacked a clear mission, adherence to accessibility standards, and was using generic stock imagery instead of real photos of participants and events. Unclear navigation also made it difficult for families of participants to find programs and the generic design made the organization lack credibility.

RESEARCH & DISCOVERY

We started by identifying accessibility gaps in the existing Culikid site, conducting competitive analysis of other nonprofit sites, and working with our client to create an updated color palette, logo, and design system.

After creating an initial prototype, we conducted a 12 participant survey through the UserTesting platform to measure mission clarity, trust, and ease of navigation of our design. After iterating on the feedback we received, we conducted A/B testing to compare two high-fidelity designs with one lighter and one darker color scheme, specifically to decide which visual direction optimized readability, warmth, and modernity.

LO-FI WIREFRAMES

As one of the main objectives of the website redesign was to clarify the information architecture and make each page more scannable by restructuring the buried navigation, we first created an updated sitemap and created low-fidelity wireframes to determine the layout of information on each page.

We then created a style guide using our client’s requested color scheme and new typography, button designs, icons, and an updated logo, which we continued to iterate on as per our client’s requests.

HI-FI ITERATIONS

We iterated on the visual system across four high-fidelity versions, each of which were created using client feedback on the previous iteration. Our client requested that we change our initial darker color scheme to be lighter for a cleaner look and remove the colored backgrounds on each section. We conducted A/B testing on the two designs and ultimately landed on a hybrid of the two color schemes for our final design.

FINAL DESIGN

OUTCOMES

We delivered 23 fully redesigned pages for handoff to the engineering team to implement with modernized visual design, restructured information architecture, more intuitive and scannable navigation, real photos and statistics to build user trust, and clear CTAs on each page.

TAKEAWAYS

Working with a client: Sometimes our design choices conflict with a client’s imagined final product, and sometimes what they picture also changes over time. It’s ultimately most important to listen to their needs and understand where they are coming from, but it’s also beneficial to remember that our decisions also have reasoning behind them and gauge whether it’s appropriate to push back against certain requests.

Designing for a purpose: Some of our earlier designs included more elaborate visual effects, a more extensive color scheme, etc. Later iterations resulting from client feedback honed in on a simpler design because we were reminded that the purpose of the product is ultimately for parents and caregivers of participants to find information easily, which could be complicated by doing too much with the design.
